Coastal change · Port Kembla

How much of Perkins Beach's dune system has been lost?

Compared historical aerial photos, photogrammetry spot heights and modern LiDAR along 69 transects to measure how far the dunes have retreated and how much sand has gone.

236 m
mean loss of dune width
~750 m³
mean sand volume lost per transect
3,782 m³
largest loss, at the northern end

Method

Built a 1961 TIN from 2,496 photogrammetry spot heights and a 2013 TIN from 5 m LiDAR. Buffered each transect and ran Polygon Volume on both surfaces, then differenced the volumes in Excel. Measured dune width separately from the 1961 aerial mosaic and 2016 WorldView-2 imagery, and compared the two approaches.

Finding

Central transects narrowed by more than 400 m, with a maximum of 548 m at Transect 61. Width loss and volume loss rose together, but not in a straight line. Transect 12 lost a lot of width and little volume, which came down to digitising choices and DEM interpolation.

Tools

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • TIN and DEM
  • LiDAR
  • Photogrammetry
  • Excel

Limits

Vegetation lines were hard to digitise on the 1961 photos, and interpolating the 1961 surface smooths small dune features.

Marine conservation planning · Five Islands Nature Reserve

Where should the sanctuary zones go around Five Islands?

Modelled kelp, seabed geomorphology and Eastern Rock Lobster habitat to recommend marine reserve zoning for the NSW Office of Environment and Heritage and the Marine Estate Management Authority.

3
habitat layers overlaid: kelp, geomorphology, lobster
2
sanctuary zones recommended
80 to 100%
predicted kelp cover on shallow reefs

Method

Ran an OLS regression in GeoDa on ten physical variables, including bathymetry, slope, rugosity, BPI and WorldView-2 bands, then applied the coefficients with Raster Calculator to produce a 0 to 100% kelp suitability surface. Classified seabed types with an unsupervised ISO Cluster. Reclassified bathymetry to the lobster's preferred depth of 8 to 13 m. Overlaid everything and digitised the zones.

Finding

The best habitat sits around Big Island and the northern slopes of Flinders Islet. Two large sanctuary zones there, inside a surrounding general use zone, protect reef, kelp and lobster habitat and stay simple to manage and enforce.

Tools

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • GeoDa
  • OLS regression
  • ISO Cluster
  • Raster Calculator
  • Bathymetry

Limits

The kelp model leaned on few video ground reference points, so field checks such as dive transects would come next.

Drone imagery classification · Big Island

What invasive vegetation covers Big Island?

Classified four band drone imagery into the weeds and surfaces that matter for invasive species management, then tested how far the map could be trusted.

77.61%
overall accuracy
94.12%
user accuracy, Kikuyu Grass
24.24%
user accuracy, Coastal Morning Glory

Method

Supervised maximum likelihood classification in ArcGIS Pro using 10 to 20 training polygons per class for Kikuyu Grass, Coastal Morning Glory, Mirror Bush, bare ground and rocky coastline. Added subclasses to handle the different tones of rock and water. Chose supervised over unsupervised so that field data, not whitewash or shadow, drove the classes.

Finding

Weeds dominate the island. Rock (100%) and bare ground (83.33%) were mapped well. Kikuyu and Morning Glory were confused with each other, so the map cannot say which one covers more area.

Tools

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • Maximum likelihood
  • Training samples
  • Error matrix

Limits

Similar spectra, limited bands, shadows and mixed pixels at edges all lowered accuracy.

Polar remote sensing · McMurdo Dry Valleys

Can vegetation indices find moss in an Antarctic protected area?

Group project annotating UAV multispectral imagery of Antarctic Specially Protected Area 131 to create training data for deep learning models, linked to the SAEF and Antarctic Science Platform monitoring programs. I wrote the individual discussion that tested the indices against the annotations.

20
fixed 1 m² quadrats annotated across student teams
93.64%
of annotated area was rock and soil
5.79%
annotated area was moss

Method

Calculated NDVI, GNDVI, MSAVI, TVI, DVI and LCI in the QGIS Raster Calculator from five band imagery. Digitised class polygons with a confidence score, guided by RTK GNSS points, quadrat photos and a Spectral Angle Mapper layer.

Finding

NDVI missed lichen, which looks bright in visible light but weak in near infrared. MSAVI separated moss from bright soil more clearly. The best workflow combined indices, field photos and semi automated help from SAM, with a human checking the result.

Tools

  • QGIS 3.40
  • ArcGIS Pro
  • Multispectral UAV imagery
  • RTK GNSS
  • Vegetation indices

Limits

Only 0.18% of the area was annotated and there was no comparison across seasons.

Bushfire analysis · NSW east coast, 2019 to 2020

What shaped fire severity in the Black Summer?

Individual report from the Fire Module, testing how rainfall, vegetation type, aspect and time since the last fire relate to burn severity across the NSW east coast.

26% vs 17%
extreme severity share on flat vs south facing land
0.075
correlation between time since last fire and severity
2016 to 2019
rainfall anomaly compared with the fires

Method

Layered fire severity with rainfall anomaly maps in ArcGIS, joined severity to vegetation types, used a DEM for elevation and aspect, and pulled time since last fire from raster band statistics. Charts and tables were made in Excel.

Finding

Fires burned most where rainfall had fallen furthest below average. Heathlands showed the highest share of extreme severity. Flat land burned hardest. Time since the last fire told us very little.

Tools

  • ArcGIS
  • DEM and aspect
  • Raster statistics
  • Excel
